DexRemo: An Open Force-Sensing Dexterous Hand Platform

Co-First AuthorDexterous ManipulationForce SensingDexterous-Hand Imitation LearningContact-Rich ManipulationReal Robot System

To address contact-state changes that are difficult to observe visually in contact-rich removal tasks, I developed an open force-sensing dexterous-hand platform. Fingertip 3D force measurements are transformed from local finger frames into a unified wrist frame, reducing representation noise caused by changing multi-finger configurations. Building on this platform, the ACT imitation-learning framework was extended to fuse vision, proprioception, fingertip pose, and force signals for closed-loop dexterous removal on a real robot.

Paper: Zhong, H., et al. An Open Force-Sensing Hand Platform and Evaluation Suite for Contact-Rich Removal Manipulation. IEEE Transactions on Robotics (T-RO), second-round review.

My contribution: Led the mechanical design of the dexterous hand, the 3D fingertip force sensors, embedded control, and system integration; proposed and implemented the wrist-frame force representation; modified ACT to fuse vision, proprioception, fingertip pose, and force; and conducted real-robot experiments and ablations across nine classes of contact-rich removal tasks.

Hidden Force-Regime Coverage: Contact-Rich Bimanual Imitation Learning

First AuthorBimanual ManipulationImitation LearningTeleoperation DemonstrationsMultimodal Perception

This work studies hidden force-regime generalization in contact-rich bimanual imitation learning, where tasks may look visually identical while differing substantially in physical resistance. I developed a programmable resistance device and a bimanual teleoperation data-collection platform, then systematically analyzed how demonstrations from different force regimes affect ACT policy generalization. The results reveal a notable downward-transfer property: demonstrations collected under higher resistance can generalize effectively to lower-resistance task conditions.

Paper: Zhong, H., et al. Hidden Force-Regime Coverage for Contact-Rich Bimanual Imitation Learning. IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA 2027), under review.

My contribution: Independently designed the programmable hidden-force-regime apparatus and bimanual contact-rich manipulation tasks; built the teleoperation demonstration and multimodal data-acquisition system; trained ACT policies combining vision, proprioception, joint torque, and fingertip force; and conducted systematic experiments on upward/downward transfer, fixed-budget mixed demonstrations, and real-bottle validation.

StrucLine-SLAM: A Fully Geometric SLAM System for Structure-Dominant Environments

First AuthorSLAMSpatial PerceptionLine FeaturesStructured Environments

For low-texture yet highly structured environments such as corridors, workshops, and photovoltaic sites, I designed a lightweight fully geometric SLAM framework that relies on line features, structural priors, and geometric constraints rather than heavy learning components, improving localization and mapping robustness in structure-dominant scenes.

Paper: Zhong, H., Wang, L., Post, M. StrucLine-SLAM: A Lightweight and Fully Geometric SLAM System for Structure-Dominant Environments. Robotics and Autonomous Systems (RAS), under review.

My contribution: Proposed the lightweight fully geometric framework; designed the line-feature extraction, structural-prior constraints, feature matching, mapping, and optimization modules; and completed implementation, public-dataset evaluation, and validation in self-collected structured environments.

MLLM-Fabric: Multimodal LLM-Driven Robotic Fabric Sorting

RA-LICRA 2026 PresentationMultimodal LLMKnowledge DistillationRobot Tactile Perception

To address the difficulty of jointly interpreting vision, tactile sensing, and pressure information for robotic fabric selection, this work develops a multimodal large-language-model framework for fabric sorting. Supervised fine-tuning, pairwise preference modeling, and explanation-guided knowledge distillation enable interpretable comparison and selection based on physical material properties.

Paper: Wang, L., Zhong, H., Wang, T., Luo, S., Zhu, J. MLLM-Fabric: Multimodal Large Language Model-Driven Robotic Framework for Fabric Sorting and Selection. IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters (RA-L), 2025.

My contribution: Contributed to multimodal fabric data collection and the robotic experimental system; designed physical-property comparison tasks, reasoning questions, and prompting workflows; participated in supervised fine-tuning, pairwise preference modeling, and explanation-guided knowledge distillation; and presented the work as an ICRA 2026 poster presenter.
